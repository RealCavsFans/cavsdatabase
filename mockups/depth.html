Idea A · Card stacks

A stack of player cards at each position

Five stacks, one per position. The card on top is the starter and the backups peek out behind him in order. Tap a stack and it deals out the whole depth at that spot. It looks like a hand of trading cards, so there's nothing to explain.

Idea B · Locker room

Jerseys hanging in their lockers

Each position is a locker with a brass nameplate. The starter's jersey hangs front and center with his name and number on the back. The backups are smaller jerseys on the bench below. Everyone knows how to read a jersey.

Idea C · On the floor

The starting five, standing at their spots

Headshot cutouts of the starters standing on a clean, light floor at their usual spots, with a shadow under each. A name tag sits below each one, and the backups line up under it as little faces. The court does the explaining.

Mockup order. The live version would follow the depth source you pick.

Idea D · The real thing

An actual depth chart

The sheet a team hands out: positions down the side, first, second and third string across, with the starter column tinted. It has faces and numbers, and nothing to figure out. The last column holds the two-way guys who can slot in at each spot.

Idea E · Pyramid

Roles, from cornerstones down

Not by position but by importance. The cornerstones sit at the top, then the rest of the starting five, the rotation, the depth, and the two-way and development players at the base. The pyramid widens as the roles get smaller and the color fades with it, so you can see how the roster is built in one look.
