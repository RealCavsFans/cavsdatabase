How we count
  • Possessions: counted play by play, the way NBA.com counts them. A possession ends on a made basket, a defensive rebound, a turnover or the end of a period; an offensive rebound continues it. Every game is checked each morning against NBA.com’s official count (pbpstats until NBA.com has it).
  • Pace: 48 × (both teams’ possessions ÷ 2) ÷ (minutes ÷ 5).
  • ORTG / DRTG / NET RTG: points scored per 100 of the Cavs’ possessions, points allowed per 100 of the opponent’s possessions, and ORTG minus DRTG.
  • TS% (true shooting): points ÷ (2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA)). eFG%: (FGM + 0.5 × 3PM) ÷ FGA. Relative TS% (rTS%): TS% minus the whole NBA’s TS% this season (every team, not by position). FTA rate: free throw attempts ÷ FGA. 3PT rate: threes ÷ FGA.
  • TOV%: turnovers per possession. AST%: assisted share of made shots. STL%: steals per 100 opponent possessions. BLK%: share of opponent twos blocked.
  • Rebounds: REB, OREB and DREB in box scores are players’ rebounds, matching the NBA box score. OREB%, DREB% and REB% use every rebound of a missed shot, team rebounds included (a miss that goes out of bounds), as NBA.com counts them.
  • Transition: a shot, foul or turnover within 7 seconds of a steal or defensive rebound; dead-ball turnovers don’t start one. It won’t always match the NBA’s fast break points, which are scored by hand.
  • Garbage time (the “No garbage time” view): 4th quarter or overtime, a lead of 25+ with 9–12 minutes left, 20+ with 6–9 left or 10+ with 6 or fewer left, and no more than two of the 10 starters on the floor. Only the last unbroken stretch to the final buzzer counts, the Cleaning the Glass rule. That view also leaves out possessions that start with under 2 seconds left in the 1st–3rd quarters. End-of-quarter heaves are never shot attempts in our counts.
  • Shot zones: at the rim within 4 ft; short midrange 4–14 ft; long midrange 14 ft to the arc; corner 3; above-the-break 3. Every shot, garbage time included.
  • Averages over several games: every rate is figured from the totals of those games (all points ÷ all possessions, all rebounds ÷ all chances), never an average of each game’s rate, so the game log, team stats and splits agree. Percentages are shown to one decimal.
  • NBA ranks: every team’s ratings, pace, rebound rates and box-score stats come from the NBA’s own box scores and play-by-play, counted the same way as the Cavs’. A few play-by-play splits (how possessions started) use pbpstats.
