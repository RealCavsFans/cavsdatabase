            CBA glossary

            The terms on this page, in plain English

            Cap lines

            Salary cap
            The main spending line, set each year from league revenue (Basketball Related Income). For 2026–27 it is $164,961,000. A team can’t go over it to sign players unless it uses an exception.
            Soft cap
            The NBA cap is ’soft’ because teams can go over it using exceptions such as Bird rights, the mid-level, or the minimum. Most teams are over the cap every year.
            Cap space (Room)
            How far a team’s salary sits below the salary cap. A team can use cap space to sign free agents or take on salary in trades without matching.
            Team salary
            The total the league measures against the cap. It includes player salaries plus cap holds, dead money and certain other charges, and it leaves out two-way contracts and some other items.
            Committed salary
            Salary a team is already obligated to pay players under signed contracts for a season, before cap holds are added. It is the core of team salary.
            Cap hit
            The amount a single player’s contract counts toward team salary in a given season. It can differ from what he is actually paid, for example with likely bonuses or veteran minimum subsidies.
            Dead money (dead salary)
            Guaranteed salary still on a team’s books for a player it waived. It counts toward team salary unless removed by set-off or a long-term injury exclusion.
            Cap hold
            A placeholder amount that counts against the cap for a team’s own free agents, unsigned first round picks, open roster spots and some exceptions. It stops teams from using cap space and then re-signing their own players over the cap.
            Free agent cap hold
            A hold on a team’s own free agent equal to a set percentage of his prior salary (for example, 190% or 150% for a Full Bird player depending on salary). It stays until the player signs or is renounced.
            First round pick cap hold
            An unsigned first round pick counts at 120% of his rookie scale amount from the moment he is drafted until he signs or his rights are lost or traded.
            Open roster spot cap hold
            If a team has fewer than 12 players counting toward the cap, each empty spot up to 12 counts as a rookie minimum salary hold.
            Renounce
            Giving up the right to re-sign a free agent (or another right) so its cap hold comes off the books and frees cap space. A renounced player can still be re-signed with cap room or the minimum, but not with Bird rights.
            Minimum team salary
            The salary floor, set at 90% of the cap: $148,465,000 for 2026–27. A team below it at the start of the regular season must pay the shortfall to its players and is charged a hold for the difference.
            Luxury tax
            A cash penalty owed by teams whose salary is above the tax line at the end of the regular season. For 2026–27 the tax line is $200,428,000, which is always 121.5% of the cap. It brings no roster restrictions by itself.
            Tax bill (tax liability)
            The amount a taxpaying team owes. It is figured in brackets, each $6,064,000 wide for 2026–27, and every dollar in a bracket is charged that bracket’s rate: $1.00 per dollar for the first $6.06M over the line, $1.25 for the next $6.06M, then $3.50, $4.75, and $0.50 more for each bracket after that. Example: a team $8.5M over owes $6.06M × $1.00 plus $2.44M × $1.25, about $9.1M. The bracket widths rise with the cap each season; these rates began in 2025–26 under the 2023 CBA (the first two seasons used $1.50, $1.75, $2.50 and $3.25). Teams that are repeaters pay more (see Repeater tax).
            Tax bracket
            Each band of salary above the tax line. Each band is one Tax Bracket Amount wide ($6,064,000 for 2026–27) and has its own tax rate.
            Taxpayer
            A team whose tax salary at the end of the regular season is above the tax line.
            Repeater tax
            Higher tax rates charged to teams that paid tax in at least 3 of the previous 4 seasons: $3.00 per dollar for the first $6.06M over the line, $3.25 for the next, then $5.50, $6.75, and $0.50 more for each bracket after that (2026–27 brackets). The same $8.5M over would cost a repeater about $26.1M.
            Tax distribution
            Up to half of all tax payments can be split among teams that didn’t pay tax. Teams that missed the salary floor don’t share.
            Tax salary
            The salary figure used for the luxury tax, measured at the start of a team’s last regular season game. It adds earned unlikely bonuses and takes out likely bonuses that weren’t earned.
            First apron
            A spending line above the tax ($209,015,000 for 2026–27). Teams above it can’t use the non-taxpayer MLE or bi-annual exception, take in a player via sign-and-trade, use an expanded TPE, or sign certain waived players, and face limits on using standard TPEs.
            Second apron
            The highest spending line ($221,686,000 for 2026–27). Teams above it also lose the taxpayer MLE, can’t combine salaries in trades, can’t send cash in trades, and risk having a future first round pick frozen.
            Apron team salary
            The salary figure used to test the aprons. It starts from team salary, drops cap holds, adds unlikely bonuses, and counts minimum deals for players with zero or one year of service at the minimum for two years of service, among other adjustments.
            Hard cap
            A ceiling a team cannot exceed for any reason for the rest of the season. A team becomes hard-capped at the first or second apron when it uses a move limited by that apron, such as the non-taxpayer MLE or a sign-and-trade (first apron) or the taxpayer MLE (second apron). The Cavs are hard-capped at the first apron after the Watson sign-and-trade.
            Payroll vs. team salary
            Payroll is what a team actually pays its players. Team salary is the cap-accounting number, which can differ because of cap holds, minimum salary subsidies, likely/unlikely bonuses and stretched dead money.
            Basketball Related Income (BRI)
            The league’s revenue base that sets the salary cap and other thresholds each year.
            Average player salary / EAPS
            The league average salary. The Estimated Average Player Salary (EAPS) is 104.5% of the prior year’s average ($15,163,000 for 2026–27) and is used in several contract formulas.
            Long-term injury exclusion
            After a player is waived and the league approves, a career-ending injury can let a team remove his salary from team salary. The salary comes back if he later plays 25 games.

            Exceptions

            Exception
            A rule that lets a team over the cap add salary by signing or trading for players. Most can be split among players, and most can’t be combined to sign one player.
            Non-taxpayer mid-level exception (NTMLE)
            Lets a team below the first apron sign free agents (or trade for or claim players) up to $15,044,000 for 2026–27, for up to 4 years. Using it hard-caps the team at the first apron.
            Taxpayer mid-level exception (TMLE)
            A smaller mid-level ($6,064,000 for 2026–27, up to 2 years) available to teams below the second apron. Using it hard-caps the team at the second apron and blocks several other moves for the year.
            Room mid-level exception (Room MLE)
            For teams that use cap space: lets them go back over the cap once by up to $9,366,000 for 2026–27, for up to 3 years. A team that uses it can’t use the other MLEs or the bi-annual.
            Bi-annual exception (BAE)
            Lets a team below the first apron sign players up to $5,477,000 for 2026–27, for up to 2 years. It can’t be used in back-to-back seasons.
            Minimum exception
            Lets any team sign players to minimum salary contracts of up to 2 years, even over the cap or aprons.
            Veteran minimum subsidy
            When a veteran signs a minimum deal for one season, the team’s cap and tax charge is only the minimum for a player with two years of service. The league pays the rest of his salary.
            Minimum salary
            The lowest salary a player can earn, set by his years of service. For 2026–27 it runs from $1,357,763 (0 years) to $3,876,529 (10+ years).
            Traded player exception (TPE)
            An umbrella term for the rules that let a team over the cap take back salary in a trade. In everyday use it usually means the leftover credit a team gets when it sends out more salary than it takes back.
            Standard TPE
            Lets a team take back up to 100% of one outgoing player’s salary plus $250,000. If it takes back less, the unused amount becomes a trade exception it can use later.
            Trade exception (TPE balance)
            Credit created when a team sends out more salary than it takes back. It can be used for one or more later trades or waiver claims, and partial use leaves the rest available until it expires. It can’t be combined with another exception or a player’s salary.
            TPE expiration
            A trade exception generally expires one year after it is created. If the team is over the first apron, a TPE created during the season can only be used after that regular season ends, and in practice expires at its end.
            Expanded TPE
            For teams below the first apron: lets them take back more salary than they send out. The limit is 200% plus $250,000 for small salaries, 100% plus a fixed cushion in the middle band, and 125% plus $250,000 for big salaries. Using it hard-caps the team at the first apron.
            Aggregated TPE
            Lets a team combine two or more outgoing salaries to take back up to 100% of the total plus $250,000. Only teams below the second apron can use it, and using it hard-caps the team there.
            Room TPE
            A team under the cap can absorb salary in a trade as long as it ends up no more than $250,000 over the cap.
            Disabled player exception (DPE)
            If the league rules that a player is likely out for the season, the team can add a replacement for one season at the lesser of half his salary or the NTMLE amount. It must be applied for by January 15 and used by March 10.
            Rookie scale exception
            Lets a team sign its first round picks to rookie scale contracts even if it’s over the cap.
            Second round pick exception
            Lets a team sign its second round picks over the cap to deals of 2 years plus a team option or 3 years plus a team option, with salaries tied to the minimum scale. These deals don’t count toward team salary until July 31.
            Bird rights (Veteran Free Agent Exception)
            Lets a team go over the cap to re-sign its own free agent. Full Bird rights (3 seasons) allow up to the max for 5 years with 8% raises.
            Early Bird rights
            After 2 seasons, a team can re-sign its player for the greater of 175% of his prior salary or 105% of the average salary ($15,235,500 for 2026–27). The deal must run 2-4 years.
            Non-Bird rights
            After 1 season, a team can re-sign its player for up to the greater of 120% of his prior salary or the minimum, for up to 4 years with 5% raises.
            Bird clock
            The count of seasons a player has spent with a team without changing teams as a free agent. Being traded or claimed off waivers generally keeps it running, and renouncing him doesn’t reset it.
            Non-aggregation of exceptions
            A team can’t combine two exceptions (say, the MLE and the BAE) to sign one player for more than either allows.
            Exception proration
            Most exceptions keep full value until January 10, then shrink day by day. The minimum prorates from the start of the season, and the DPE and TPEs don’t prorate.

            Players’ rights & free agency

            Unrestricted free agent (UFA)
            A player not under contract who can sign with any team. His old team has no right to match offers.
            Restricted free agent (RFA)
            A free agent whose former team can match any offer sheet he signs. It usually applies to players coming off rookie scale deals or with 3 or fewer years of service, as long as the team made a qualifying offer.
            Qualifying offer (QO)
            A fully guaranteed offer for one season that a team must make by June 29 to keep a player restricted. The amount is set by a formula tied to draft slot and starter criteria. The player can accept it and become a UFA after that season.
            Starter criteria
            A player meets them by starting 41 games or playing 2,000 minutes in the prior season, or averaging that over the last two. Meeting them can raise or lower the size of his qualifying offer.
            Maximum qualifying offer
            An optional qualifying offer of five seasons at the max salary a team can make to a player coming off his rookie scale. The player may accept it instead of the standard QO.
            Offer sheet
            A contract another team signs with a restricted free agent. It must run at least 2 years (3 against a maximum QO), and the offering team needs cap room or an exception to cover it.
            Right of first refusal (matching)
            The former team’s right to keep a restricted free agent by matching an offer sheet’s principal terms, usually within about a day or two. A matched player can’t be traded for a year without his consent, and never to the offering team in that year.
            Gilbert Arenas provision
            Limits offer sheets to RFAs with 1 or 2 years of service. The first year can’t exceed the non-taxpayer MLE, though the offer can jump in year 3. The matching team can spread the cap hit evenly.
            Moratorium
            The early-July quiet period (July 1 through noon on July 6) when deals can be agreed to but most can’t be signed.
            Prior team
            The team whose roster a player finished the season on. It’s the only team that can use his Bird rights.
            Rookie free agent
            An eligible player who went undrafted, or whose draft rights lapsed. He can sign with any team.
            Years of service (YOS)
            A player earns a year of service for each season he spends at least one regular season day on an NBA active or inactive list. YOS sets minimum and maximum salaries and two-way eligibility.
            Draft rights
            A team’s exclusive right to negotiate with a player it drafted who hasn’t signed yet.
            Required tender
            A contract offer for one season that a team must make to its draft pick (by July 15 for first rounders, August 5 for second rounders) to keep his draft rights.

            Contracts

            Pay rank
            Used on this site’s cap sheet: where a player’s average annual salary ranks among all NBA players at his position, using Spotrac’s active contracts.
            Uniform Player Contract (UPC)
            The standard contract every player signs. Teams and players modify it only through optional exhibits, such as guarantees, options and trade bonuses.
            Standard contract
            Any NBA contract other than a two-way. Only standard contracts count toward the 15-man roster.
            Rookie scale contract
            The four season deal every first round pick signs. The first 2 years are guaranteed and years 3 and 4 are team options. Pay is set by draft slot, from 80% to 120% of the scale amount.
            Rookie scale team options
            The third- and fourth-year options on a rookie scale deal. Each must be picked up by October 31 of the season before it applies. If one is declined, the player becomes an unrestricted free agent, and his old team can pay him no more than the option amount.
            Maximum salary
            The most a player can earn in the first year of a contract, set by years of service. For 2026–27 it is $41,240,250 for 0-6 years (25% of the cap), $49,488,300 for 7-9 years (30%), and $57,736,350 for 10+ years (35%).
            Higher max criteria
            Winning MVP, making All-NBA or winning DPOY in the right seasons. Meeting them lets a player earn a higher max percentage, such as 30% instead of 25% on a rookie extension, or 35% instead of 30%.
            Designated veteran extension (supermax)
            An extension of up to six seasons at 30–35% of the cap for a player with 7 or 8 years of service who has stayed with his drafting team (or was traded in his first 4 years) and met the higher max criteria. A player who signs one can’t be traded for a year.
            Rookie scale extension
            An extension a first round pick can sign after his third season, before his fourth season starts. It pays up to 25% of the cap (30% if he meets the higher max criteria) for up to 5 extra years.
            Veteran extension
            An extension for a player not on a rookie deal. The first year is generally capped at 140% of his current salary or 140% of the EAPS, with up to 5 total seasons and 8% raises.
            Extension eligibility
            A veteran can extend 2 years after signing a deal of three or four seasons, or 3 years after signing a deal of five or six seasons. A team that trades for a player must wait 6 months before giving him a big extension.
            Renegotiation
            A team with cap space raises a player’s current season salary. It needs a 4+ year contract at least 3 years old, can’t be done from March 1 to June 30, and the player can’t be traded for 6 months afterward.
            Raises
            Yearly salary increases are capped at 8% of the first-year salary for Bird-rights re-signings and extensions, and 5% for most other signings.
            Player option
            An option the player controls to extend his contract for one more season. It generally must be decided by June 29.
            Team option
            An option the team controls to extend a player’s contract for one more season. It generally must be decided by June 29 (October 31 for rookie scale options).
            Early termination option (ETO)
            A player’s right to end his contract one year early. It can only apply after the fourth season.
            Guaranteed contract
            A contract with full compensation protection: the player gets his full salary even if waived.
            Partially guaranteed contract
            A contract where only part of the salary is protected if the player is waived.
            Non-guaranteed contract
            A contract with no compensation protection. The team owes nothing beyond days already earned if it waives him early enough.
            Compensation protection
            The contract language that guarantees some or all of a player’s salary (for lack of skill, injury and so on) if the team ends the contract early.
            January 10 guarantee date
            Non-guaranteed contracts become fully guaranteed for the season if the player is still under contract on January 10. Teams must waive such players by about January 7 so they clear waivers in time.
            Likely / unlikely bonuses
            Incentives are ’likely’ if they would have been met the prior season, and those count toward team salary. ’Unlikely’ incentives don’t count toward the cap but do count for the aprons and are added at season’s end if earned.
            Incentive limits
            Total incentives can’t exceed 20% of a player’s regular salary, and unlikely bonuses 15%.
            Signing bonus
            Up-front pay of up to 15% of a contract (10% on offer sheets). For cap purposes it is spread evenly over the contract years.
            Deferred compensation
            Salary earned in a season but paid after May 1, capped at 25% of that season’s pay. It counts against the cap in the season it is earned.
            Two-way contract
            A contract of up to 2 years for players with fewer than 4 years of service. It pays half the rookie minimum ($678,882 for 2026–27) and doesn’t count toward team salary. Players can be active for up to 50 NBA games. Teams can carry 3.
            Two-way three season limit
            A team can’t sign, convert or acquire a player who has already been on a two-way with that team during parts of more than 3 seasons.
            Two-way conversion
            A team can convert a two-way to a standard contract (paying the minimum, same length) any time before its last regular season game without the player’s consent. Two-way players aren’t playoff-eligible unless converted before the regular season ends.
            Under-fifteen games
            Games where a team has fewer than 15 standard contracts and a two-way player is active. A team is limited to 90 per season.
            Exhibit 9 (training camp contract)
            A training camp contract for one season at the minimum (or two-way salary) with no guarantee of any kind. It lets a team bring a player to camp to compete for a spot at little financial risk.
            Exhibit 10
            A minimum contract for one season that can be converted to a two-way before the regular season. It can include a bonus the player earns if he is waived and then spends 60 days with the team’s G League affiliate. Teams can have 6 at once.
            Exhibit 10 bonus
            A bonus of $5,000 to $91,000 (2026–27 maximum). It is paid if the player is waived before the season, signs with the team’s G League affiliate, and stays there 60 days. It doesn’t count toward team salary.
            Affiliate player
            A player waived from an Exhibit 10 deal who then joins the NBA team’s G League affiliate, which keeps his G League rights. A G League team that holds his returning-player rights takes priority.
            Summer contract
            A training camp contract signed between July 1 and the start of the season. It pays nothing until the regular season and doesn’t count toward team salary until then.
            10-day contract
            A contract lasting 10 days or 3 games, whichever is longer, at a prorated minimum. It can be signed from January 5 (earlier under hardship). A team can give the same player only two before signing him for the rest of the season.
            Rest-of-season contract
            A standard contract signed after the regular season begins (other than a 10-day). Salary is prorated for days remaining.
            Over-38 rule
            Limits how much salary in later years can be counted for players who would be 38 or older at the end of a contract.

            Trades

            Salary matching
            Teams over the cap must take back salary within set limits based on what they send out. Generally that is up to 100% plus $250,000, or more using the expanded TPE if below the first apron. Teams over the first apron lose the $250,000 cushion.
            Trade salary
            How much a player’s salary counts in trade matching. It can differ from his cap hit. For example, non-guaranteed salary counts only partly as outgoing salary early in the season, and minimum deals count as $0 incoming.
            Aggregation
            Combining two or more players’ salaries in one trade to match a larger salary. Teams above the second apron can’t do it.
            Two-month aggregation restriction
            A player acquired in a trade can’t be combined with other salaries in another trade for 2 months. The exception is a player acquired before December 17, who can be aggregated at the deadline.
            Newly signed player trade restriction
            Most free agents signed in the offseason can’t be traded until the later of 3 months or December 15. Players re-signed with Bird rights for a raise of more than 20% wait until the later of 3 months or January 15.
            Base year compensation
            When a team re-signs its own player with Bird rights for a big raise and trades him soon after (including sign-and-trades), his outgoing trade salary can count lower than his cap hit. The usual 3-month/January 15 trade restriction also applies.
            Poison pill
            Applies when a player who just signed a rookie scale extension is traded before it kicks in. The receiving team counts him at the average salary of his full contract, while the sending team counts his current salary.
            Cash in trades
            Teams can send and receive up to 5.15% of the cap in cash each season ($8,495,000 for 2026–27), tracked separately in each direction. Teams above the second apron can’t send cash.
            Trade kicker (trade bonus)
            A bonus of up to 15% of remaining salary paid when a player is traded. The sending team pays it, but it counts on the receiving team’s books. It’s paid only once, and the player can waive it.
            Sign-and-trade
            A team re-signs its own free agent using Bird rights and immediately trades him. The deal must run 3-4 years and be fully guaranteed in year 1, and it must be done before the season starts. The receiving team becomes hard-capped at the first apron.
            Extend-and-trade
            A player signs an extension and is traded in the same move. It is limited to 120% of his prior salary, 4 total seasons and 5% raises.
            No-trade clause
            A player’s right to block a trade. Express clauses are only allowed for players with 8+ years of service and 4+ with the same team. An automatic one season clause applies after some deals of one season.
            Trade deadline
            After the deadline (early February), no trades can be made until the team’s regular season ends.
            Touch rule
            In trades involving three or more teams, every team must send or receive something from at least two others.
            Stepien Rule
            A team can’t leave itself without a first round pick in two consecutive future drafts. Owning another team’s first that year satisfies the rule.
            Seven year rule
            Picks can only be traded up to seven drafts in the future.
            Frozen pick
            If a team finishes the regular season above the second apron, its first round pick seven years out becomes untradeable.
            Pick slide
            A frozen first round pick moves to the end of the first round if the team is above the second apron in 2 of the next 4 seasons. Otherwise it unfreezes.

            Draft & picks

            Protected pick
            A pick that only conveys if it lands outside a set range (for example, top-10 protected). If it doesn’t convey, the trade spells out what happens next, such as rolling to a later year. Protections must still meet the Stepien Rule.
            Conditional pick
            A pick that conveys only if stated conditions are met. Specific contingency language is used so it still meets the Stepien and seven year rules.
            Pick deferral
            A one-time option to delay a pick’s conveyance or receipt by one year. It can’t be combined with protections on the same pick.
            Pick swap
            The right to exchange picks with another team in a given draft if the other team’s pick is better.
            Most / least favorable
            Language in a trade where a team receives the better (most favorable) or worse (least favorable) of two or more teams’ picks in a given draft.
            Draft-and-stash
            Drafting a player, usually an international one, who keeps playing abroad. The team keeps his rights while he doesn’t count against the cap, and his non-NBA contract extends the rights.
            Trading a pick vs. a drafted player
            A pick can be traded until 2 p.m. ET on draft day. After the pick is made, the team holds that player’s draft rights, which can be traded right away and count as $0 in salary matching. Once the player signs, he can’t be traded for 30 days.
            Draft-and-trade
            A team picks a player on behalf of another team and trades his draft rights after the selection. This is common because rights can be moved right away and count as $0.
            Early entrant
            An underclassman who enters the draft. He can withdraw up to 10 days before the draft, but not from more than two drafts.
            Draft eligibility
            A player must turn 19 during the draft year and be a year out of high school. International players don’t need to declare to be eligible.

            Roster & waivers

            Roster limits
            During the season a team must carry 14-15 standard contracts plus up to 3 two-ways. In the offseason it can carry up to 21, including two-ways.
            14-man minimum
            A team can drop to 12 or 13 standard players for up to two weeks at a time and 28 days total in a season. Otherwise it needs at least 14.
            Active / inactive list
            Players on standard contracts are on either the active list (12-15 players during the season) or the inactive list. Two-way players move onto these lists when they’re with the NBA team.
            Hardship exception
            A team with many injured players can sign extra players (usually on 10-day deals) above the 15-man limit.
            G League assignment
            A team can send a player with fewer than 3 years of service to the G League without his consent. Veterans must agree.
            Waivers
            How a team releases a player under contract. He sits on waivers for 48 hours, during which other teams can claim him. If unclaimed, he becomes a free agent and is still owed his guaranteed money.
            Waiver claim
            A team with room or an exception big enough for the contract can claim a waived player and take on his whole contract. The worst record gets priority, and the claimed player can’t be traded for 30 days.
            Stretch provision
            When a team waives a player, it can spread his remaining guaranteed money over twice the remaining years plus one. It can’t exceed 15% of the cap in any season.
            Set-off
            If a waived player signs elsewhere, his old team’s dead money is reduced by half of what his new salary exceeds the minimum.
            Buyout
            A player agrees to give up part of his guaranteed salary in exchange for his release. To be playoff-eligible with a new team, he must be waived by March 1.
            March 1 playoff-eligibility deadline
            A player waived after March 1 can’t play in the playoffs for a new team.
            Universal cutdown date
            The deadline before the regular season (5 p.m. two days before opening night) to waive non-guaranteed players without any of their salary counting.
            Training camp roster
            In the offseason a team can carry up to 21 players, including camp invites on Exhibit 9 or 10 deals and two-ways.
            Suspended list
            A suspended player can be moved off the roster count after the 5th game of a league suspension (or 3rd of a team suspension).

            Figures are 2026–27. Simplified from the CBA Guide, the NBA's CBA 101, Larry Coon's CBA FAQ, the Hoops Rumors glossary and Salary Swish. The CBA has exceptions to nearly every rule; those sources have the fine print.
